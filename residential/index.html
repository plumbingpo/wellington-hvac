Clear Pricing

Clear Pricing Before Any Work Starts

Before we touch a tool, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check the age and tonnage of your system, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a weak capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

A lot of HVAC failures send small signals well before the system stops. Watch for energy bills that creep up, weak airflow at the registers, frost on the copper lines, or a bedroom that never catches up to the thermostat. Behind those symptoms are usually pinhole refrigerant leaks, worn blower bearings, or a capacitor on its way out. A quick inspection at the first sign often costs far less than a failed compressor.

Not every call is an emergency. Plenty of Wellington homeowners ask us to evaluate an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, replacing on your own schedule beats a rushed swap in the middle of a heat wave. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your equipment has left.

Seasonal Tune-Ups

Seasonal HVAC Maintenance Plans

Each maintenance visit from Wellington HVAC is more than a filter change. The technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, puts in a fresh filter, and measures static pressure across the ducts. Small problems are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and the equipment keeps running at full efficiency.

Most homes do best with two visits a year: a spring tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. The schedule flexes with the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and local conditions like dust and pollen, which coat condenser coils and raise energy use fast.

Consider maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already bought. Each tune-up keeps the coils clean, the charge correct, and the filter fresh, which lowers monthly energy bills while easing the strain on the compressor and blower. For most families, a plan costs far less than replacing the system ahead of schedule.

Proper Sizing

Comfort Sized to Your Home's Demand

Bigger isn't better when it comes to HVAC. An oversized unit short-cycles, wastes energy, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out of the air, while an undersized one runs nonstop and still can't keep up on the hottest days. We don't simply copy the tonnage of the old unit. We run a proper load calculation that factors in your square footage, insulation, windows, and ductwork, and for mini-splits we size each head to the room it serves.

We walk you through the options for most homes, especially growing families with rising comfort demands. A single-stage system that served a small household rarely satisfies a finished basement and two added bedrooms, and a modern two-stage or variable-speed heat pump can hold tighter temperatures while cutting energy use significantly. If you're weighing furnace-and-AC against a heat pump, or ducted against ductless, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: SEER2 efficiency ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting requirements, and upfront installation work, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Common Problems

The HVAC Repairs Wellington Homes Need Most

No cooling, a furnace that won't ignite, a frozen evaporator coil, a slow refrigerant leak: each of these problems has a different cause and needs a different repair. Wellington HVAC diagnoses the exact fault before replacing anything, whether it's a condenser that hums but won't start, a short-cycling furnace, a thermostat that keeps losing its program, a clogged condensate drain, or a mini-split throwing error codes.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A weak capacitor simply keeps the compressor from starting, but a cracked heat exchanger can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a blocked condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling. We match the fix to the failure: capacitors, contactors, and fan motors on cooling systems, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards on furnaces, sealed and repaired ductwork where conditioned air is bleeding into the attic, and refrigerant recovered and recharged after a repaired leak. Whatever you're facing, our team has the parts and experience to handle it.
